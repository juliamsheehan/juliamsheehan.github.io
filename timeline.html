🗓️ Timeline of Big Updates

The reason AI feels like it's changing weekly: a lot genuinely did happen fast. Here's the through-line, with the moments that actually mattered marked as milestone. Blue dots are the era-defining ones.

💡 In plain English

The story in one sentence: a 2017 research idea (the Transformer) made models scale, scaling made them shockingly capable, ChatGPT (2022) put that in everyone's hands, and by 2024–2025 we stopped just chatting with models and started letting them act — that's the agent era we're in now.

Before 2017

Hand-written rules & early machine learning

Software “intelligence” meant rules people coded by hand, plus narrow ML for things like spam filters and recommendations. Useful, but nothing that felt like it understood language.

2017 · milestone

The Transformer — “Attention Is All You Need”

A Google research paper introduced the Transformer, an architecture that reads a whole sequence at once and learns which words to pay attention to. It was fast to train at scale — and it quietly became the foundation of every major model since. The “GPT” in ChatGPT stands for Generative Pre-trained Transformer.

2018 – 2019

BERT & GPT-2 — scaling starts to pay off

Researchers showed that pre-training a Transformer on huge text, then adapting it, beat everything before it. GPT-2 wrote paragraphs coherent enough that its release was staged out of caution. The recipe was now clear: make it bigger, feed it more.

2020

GPT-3 — big enough to be surprising

With 175 billion parameters, GPT-3 could write code, essays, and emails from a plain instruction, and learn a task from a couple of examples in the prompt (few-shot). Developers got API access and the first wave of “AI wrapper” apps appeared.

2021

GitHub Copilot — AI enters the editor

Built on an OpenAI code model, Copilot put grey “ghost-text” autocomplete right in the IDE. For many developers this was the first genuinely useful AI in daily work: press Tab, accept a suggestion, keep going.

Nov 2022 · milestone

ChatGPT — the moment everyone noticed

OpenAI wrapped a fine-tuned GPT-3.5 in a simple chat box. The conversation format + RLHF tuning made it feel helpful and safe, and it reached 100 million users faster than any app in history. This is the moment AI went from “researcher toy” to “thing your relatives ask you about.”

2023

GPT-4, open models & the RAG boom

A dense year:

  • GPT-4 and rivals (Anthropic's Claude, Google's Bard/Gemini) raised the quality bar and started accepting images.
  • Open-weight models (Meta's Llama 2, Mistral) let anyone run capable models themselves.
  • RAG and vector databases exploded as the practical way to put private data to work.
  • Function calling arrived — models could now trigger real tools, the seed of agents.
2024 · milestone

The year of agents (and much bigger context)

Tool use matured into agents — models running in a loop to complete multi-step tasks on their own. The buzz was everywhere; “agentic” became the word of the year.

  • Coding agents arrived: tools like Cursor, Devin, and agent modes that edit many files, run code, and fix their own errors — not just autocomplete a line.
  • Context windows jumped to hundreds of thousands, then a million+ tokens — paste a whole codebase.
  • Models got faster and cheaper, and small ones ran on laptops and phones.
  • Multimodal went mainstream: talk to your AI, share your screen, get voice back.
2025 · milestone

Reasoning models & agents become normal tooling

Agents moved from demo to daily driver, and two ideas standardized the ecosystem:

  • Reasoning models (“think before answering”) became a standard option, tackling hard debugging, math, and planning far better.
  • Agentic coding in the terminal & IDE (e.g. Claude Code, Cursor's agent, Copilot agents) — you hand over a goal and review the result, working in git.
  • MCP (Model Context Protocol) emerged as a common “USB-C port” standard for connecting models to tools and data sources, so integrations stopped being one-off.
  • Computer use — agents that click and type in a real screen — went from experiment to early product.
2026 · you are here

Agents as coworkers, focus on trust

The frontier now is less “can it?” and more “can I trust and direct it?” Themes to watch: multiple agents collaborating, longer autonomous tasks, tighter permission controls and audit trails, and stronger on-device models for privacy. The core skills for you haven't changed: give good context, keep a human in the loop, and verify the output.

✅ Tip

You don't need to chase every release. The categories here (chat → RAG → tools → agents → reasoning) change slowly; only the model names and scores change fast. Learn the categories once — this site — and each new announcement just slots into a box you already understand.
