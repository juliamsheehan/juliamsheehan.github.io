Lessons · one topic at a time

Pick a topic. Learn it properly.

The rest of this site is a reference you dip into. Lessons are different — each one takes a single topic and walks you all the way through it, in the same order every time, so your brain knows what's coming next and can spend its energy on the actual idea.

How every lesson is built

Each new idea in a lesson is introduced the same eight ways, in this order — explanation first, then progressively less help, until you're doing it on your own:

  1. 💡 In plain English — what the thing is, in layman's terms. No jargon, no “why it matters” yet.
  2. 🔗 Analogy — the same idea compared to something ordinary you already understand.
  3. Why it matters — only now, once the idea has landed, what it actually buys you.
  4. A diagram — a simple picture, whenever the idea has any shape or structure to it.
  5. A quick check — one small question. Pick any answer and you'll get feedback written for that specific answer, including what the misconception behind a wrong one is.
  6. 🔍 Worked example — someone doing it correctly, step by step, before you're asked to do anything.
  7. 🧩 Now you finish one — the same kind of task with the middle taken out for you to supply. Never a jump straight from a finished example to a blank page, because that gap is where people stall.
  8. ✏️ A practice task — the real attempt, on your own. See below for how those work.
💡 In plain English

Read top to bottom. Sections build on each other, so the fourth one assumes you read the first three. Answer the checkpoint questions as you go rather than skipping them — getting one wrong on purpose is one of the faster ways to find out what you actually believe.

✅ Practice tasks: pick guided or solo, and switch whenever you like

Every practice task offers both routes at once, and you're never asked to rate your own experience first — nobody can see their own gaps, and a wrong guess sends the whole thing off in the wrong direction.

  • “I'll do it myself” — the task, plus a ladder of hints that get more specific each time you press for one, and the full answer hidden underneath when you want it.
  • “Guide me” — the same task broken into steps, revealed one at a time. Each step is a real decision with feedback written for whichever option you pick, and later steps refer back to what you already worked out.

You can move between the two mid-attempt, in either direction, and nothing you've done is thrown away — so neither route is a dead end.

✅ Tip

Every lesson ends with a bigger “Test your understanding” quiz covering the whole thing, with questions deliberately spread across recalling an idea, applying it to a situation the lesson never mentioned, and analysing what breaks and why. Your score bar sits at the top of the page and is remembered in your browser, so you can leave halfway through and come back later — and if you've been away a while, the lesson offers you a quick re-test rather than a re-read.
